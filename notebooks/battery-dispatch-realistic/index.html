Stage 1b — Realistic Battery Dispatch (Efficiency + Degradation)

Stage 1a established the theoretical upper bound: perfect-foresight arbitrage with no physical losses. Stage 1b adds two sources of revenue erosion:

  1. Round-trip efficiency η_rt = 90 % — 10 % of stored energy is lost on every charge/discharge cycle. A single signed net-flow variable is no longer valid once η < 1; separate charge and discharge variables are required.

  2. Degradation cost — cycling wears the battery. Modelled as a linear EUR/kWh penalty on energy stored, parameterised by a cost per equivalent full cycle.

Three fixed scenarios are compared, then the degradation cost is swept to answer: at what per-cycle cost does day-ahead arbitrage stop clearing its own wear?

ideal1.00Stage 1a reference (daily reset)

eta90_deg0.910 EUR/cycleCombined physical realism

1. LP Formulation¶

With ηrt<1\eta_{rt} < 1ηrt​<1, separate charge ct≥0c_t \ge 0ct​≥0 and discharge dt≥0d_t \ge 0dt​≥0 variables (both in kW at the grid boundary) are needed:

SoCt+1=SoCt+ηc ct−dtηd\text{SoC}_{t+1} = \text{SoC}_t + \eta_c \, c_t - \frac{d_t}{\eta_d}SoCt+1​=SoCt​+ηc​ct​−ηd​dt​​
(1)

where ηc=ηd=ηrt\eta_c = \eta_d = \sqrt{\eta_{rt}}ηc​=ηd​=ηrt​​ (symmetric split of round-trip losses). Physical constraints:

0≤ct≤Pmax⁡,0≤dt≤Pmax⁡,0≤SoCt≤C0 \le c_t \le P_{\max}, \quad 0 \le d_t \le P_{\max}, \quad 0 \le \text{SoC}_t \le C0≤ct​≤Pmax​,0≤dt​≤Pmax​,0≤SoCt​≤C
(2)

Objective — maximise revenue minus degradation cost:

max⁡∑t[Pt1000(dt−ct)−κdeg⋅ηc⋅ct]\max \sum_t \left[\frac{P_t}{1000}(d_t - c_t) - \kappa_{\text{deg}} \cdot \eta_c \cdot c_t\right]maxt∑​[1000Pt​​(dt​−ct​)−κdeg​⋅ηc​⋅ct​]
(3)

κdeg\kappa_{\text{deg}}κdeg​ (EUR/kWh stored) relates to a per-cycle cost Δ\DeltaΔ via κdeg=Δ/C\kappa_{\text{deg}} = \Delta / Cκdeg​=Δ/C.

Break-even spread (approximate, ignoring asymmetric buy/sell price levels): a cycle is profitable when

ηrt⋅Psell−Pbuy  >  κdeg⋅ηc⋅10001 EUR/MWh\eta_{rt} \cdot P_{\text{sell}} - P_{\text{buy}} \;>\; \frac{\kappa_{\text{deg}} \cdot \eta_c \cdot 1000}{1} \text{ EUR/MWh}ηrt​⋅Psell​−Pbuy​>1κdeg​⋅ηc​⋅1000​ EUR/MWh
(4)

For ηrt=0.9\eta_{rt} = 0.9ηrt​=0.9, Δ=10\Delta = 10Δ=10 EUR/cycle this hurdle is roughly 95 EUR/MWh — present in the 2022 crisis but rare in normal years.

9. Free Horizon vs Daily Reset¶

The daily-reset constraint forces SoC = 0 at midnight every day. Idle days have SoC = 0 throughout because there is no carry-over from the previous day. With high degradation costs — where only exceptional price spreads justify a cycle — the free-horizon formulation can accumulate charge over multiple days and wait for the best opportunity, while daily-reset must start fresh each morning with an empty battery. We expect the FH premium to grow with degradation cost.
